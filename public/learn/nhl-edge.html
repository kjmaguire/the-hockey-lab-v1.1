NHL Edge tracking, explained

Sensors in the puck and in every player's sweater turned the live game into a stream of numbers. Here's what NHL Edge actually measures — skating speed, shot speed, distance and shift data — and how to read it. For the wider vocabulary, start with the analytics glossary.

Skating speed & top speed

Miles per hour · per shift, per game

Skating speed is exactly what it sounds like — how fast a player is moving — sampled continuously. The headline number is top speed, the fastest a skater hit on a given shift or game. The league's quickest players touch roughly 24–25 mph on a breakaway or rush; defensemen activating off the blue line post eye-catching numbers too.

A related counter, the 20+ mph burst, tallies how often a skater crosses that high-speed threshold in a game — a measure of explosiveness and how relentlessly a player attacks open ice, not just one peak reading.

Distance skated

Miles · per game, per shift

Distance skated totals the ground a player covers. Over a full game a busy forward can travel several miles; across both teams the combined figure climbs fast. Distance pairs naturally with speed and shift data: a player logging big mileage on short, high-speed shifts is being used very differently from one grinding out long, lower-tempo minutes.

Shot speed

Miles per hour · the puck off the stick

Shot speed measures how fast the puck leaves the stick. Tracked shots routinely sit in the 80s and 90s; the hardest one-timers from the league's biggest shooters approach and sometimes pass 100 mph. It's a fun number on its own, but it also adds context to shot quality — a blistering one-timer from the slot is a very different problem for a goalie than a wrister from the wall.

On a game center you'll often see the hardest shot and top skating speed of the night surfaced as live highlights — the moments tracking data makes legible in real time.

Shifts, time on ice & zone time

Seconds · usage and deployment

Tracking also sharpens the basics. Shift length and time on ice show how a coach is deploying a player; zone time breaks down how much of a shift was spent in the offensive, neutral and defensive zones. Together they explain the why behind a box score — whether a quiet night was about role and matchups rather than effort.

Why it matters

Reading the tracking era

On their own, speed and distance are trivia. Their value is context: they turn "he looked fast" into a number, reveal which defensemen can truly defend the rush, and show when a team on a back-to-back is a step slow. Combined with shot-quality models like expected goals, tracking data is how the modern game gets measured beyond the scoreboard.

Common questions

How fast do NHL players skate?

NHL Edge regularly clocks top speeds above 22 mph, with the fastest skaters reaching roughly 24–25 mph on a burst. A "20+ mph burst" counter tracks how often a skater crosses that threshold in a game.

How fast is an NHL shot?

Tracked shot speeds commonly reach the 90s of miles per hour, with the hardest one-timers from elite shooters approaching and occasionally passing 100 mph. Edge reports the speed the puck leaves the stick.

What does NHL Edge measure?

Skating speed and top speed, total distance skated, shot speed, shift length, time on ice and on-ice positioning — captured by sensors in the puck and players' sweaters, sampled many times per second.
