Hockey analytics, explained

Modern hockey is measured in more than goals. This is a plain-English guide to the numbers behind the game — the shot-quality models, possession proxies and tracking metrics you'll see across The Hockey Lab. No spreadsheets required.

Expected goals (xG)

Shot quality · per shot, per game, per season

Expected goals answer a simple question: how likely was that shot to go in? A model assigns every unblocked shot a scoring probability from the things that actually matter — distance from the net, angle, shot type, whether it came off a rebound or an odd-man rush, and the game situation.

Add up those probabilities and you get a team's xG for a game. A side that generates 3.2 xG created chances worth a little more than three goals, regardless of how many actually went in. Comparing xG to real goals separates finishing and goaltending luck from the underlying quality of chances.

Corsi & Fenwick

Possession proxy · shot-attempt share

Corsi counts every shot attempt — shots on goal, misses and blocks — for and against while a player or team is on the ice. Fenwick is the same idea but drops blocked shots. Neither is about scoring; both are stand-ins for which team is spending more time with the puck in the offensive zone.

They're usually shown as a percentage. A 55% Corsi share (often written CF%) means a team took 55% of all attempts in its minutes — a sign it controlled play. At 5-on-5, sustained possession tends to lead to goals, which is why these "shot-attempt" numbers became the entry point to hockey analytics.

High-danger chances

Chance quality · the slot

Not all shots are equal. A high-danger chance is an attempt from the most dangerous real estate on the ice — the slot, in close, where goals come from most often. Tracking how many high-danger chances a team creates and allows says more about who's actually threatening to score than raw shot totals do.

When you see a goalie's high-danger save percentage, that's the share of those premium chances they stopped — the truest test of a goaltender on a given night.

PDO

Luck check · shooting % + save %

PDO adds a team's shooting percentage to its save percentage at even strength. Because one team's shot is the other team's save chance, the league average is always almost exactly 100 (sometimes written 1.000). A team running well above 100 is usually getting hot shooting or goaltending that won't last; well below tends to bounce back. It's a quick gut-check for whether results are sustainable.

Zone starts

Usage · where shifts begin

Zone starts describe where a player's shifts begin on faceoffs — offensive, neutral or defensive zone. A player buried with defensive-zone starts has a harder assignment than a teammate launched into the offensive zone, so zone usage adds crucial context to every other number. It's the difference between a stat and a fair comparison.

Save percentage & GSAx

Goaltending · stops vs. expectation

Save percentage (SV%) is the share of shots a goalie stops — .910 means 91 of every 100. Useful, but it treats a harmless point shot and a point-blank rebound the same. Goals saved above expected (GSAx) fixes that: it compares goals allowed to the expected goals (xG) a goalie faced. A positive GSAx means they stopped more than an average goalie would have, given the quality of shots — the modern standard for valuing goaltending.

NHL Edge tracking

Player & puck tracking · live

The newest layer of data comes from the puck and the players themselves. NHL Edge uses sensors to capture skating speed, distance skated, shot speed, shift lengths and on-ice positioning many times per second — turning a live game into a measurable stream. It's how you learn a defenseman hit 24 mph on a breakout or that a shot left the stick at 98 mph.

We lean on Edge data throughout the app's game centers and player pages. For a full breakdown of what it measures and how to read it, see NHL Edge tracking, explained.

Common questions

What are expected goals (xG)?

Expected goals estimate the probability that a given shot becomes a goal, based on factors like shot location, distance, angle, shot type and whether it followed a rebound or a rush. Summing every shot's probability gives a team's expected goals — a measure of chance quality, not just quantity.

What's the difference between Corsi and Fenwick?

Corsi counts all shot attempts — shots on goal, missed shots and blocked shots. Fenwick is the same but excludes blocks. Both proxy for puck possession and are usually shown as a percentage of total attempts while a player or team is on the ice.

What is a high-danger scoring chance?

A shot attempt from the most dangerous areas — typically the slot, directly in front of the net — where the probability of scoring is highest. High-danger save percentage measures how often a goalie stops those premium chances.

What is NHL Edge tracking?

NHL Edge is the league's player and puck tracking system. Sensors in the puck and on player sweaters capture skating speed, distance, shot speed, shift length and positioning many times per second, turning live games into measurable data.
